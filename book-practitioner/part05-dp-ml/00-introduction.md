# Differential privacy in machine learning

This part is for anyone who trains, ships or reviews a model under differential privacy (DP). It needs the Gaussian mechanism and composition from [Part 3](../part03-dp-foundations/00-introduction.md) and an accountant from [Part 4](../part04-privacy-accounting/00-introduction.md). Its audits also reuse the membership-inference attacks of [Part 2](../part02-attacks/10-risk-assessment.md).

Every private training method has two halves. The privacy half is computed. The main algorithm is stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with clipped per-example gradients and Gaussian noise, called DP-SGD. Its accountant derives $\varepsilon$ from the noise multiplier, the sampling rate and the number of steps alone. The utility half, accuracy, is measured, not proved. So every page asks two questions: is the $\varepsilon$ correct, and is the accuracy at that $\varepsilon$ good enough?

Three pages are workflows, each a whole job done with real libraries on a public dataset. [Train a model with DP-SGD](13-train-dp-sgd.md) trains a classifier on the Adult census data with Opacus, from the privacy unit to the run record. [Fine-tune a language model privately with LoRA](14-private-llm-finetuning.md) adapts a small public language model to a private text and checks whether it memorized planted secrets. [Release and governance](12-release-governance.md) closes the part: it recomputes the guarantee, writes it down, maps it to the law and lists what to check before a model ships.

The other pages explain the decisions those jobs make, in the order a project meets them. [DP-SGD](02-dp-sgd.md) comes first, after a short reference page on [private empirical risk minimization](01-dp-erm.md) for convex models. Then come [hyperparameter tuning](03-hyperparameter-tuning.md), which charges the search to the budget, the privacy unit in [User-level differential privacy](07-user-level.md), and the budget itself in [Choosing ε](09-choosing-epsilon.md). [Public data and private fine-tuning](06-public-data.md) explains why starting from a public model helps so much. [Disparate impact](08-disparate-impact.md) and [Auditing private training](10-auditing.md) are the checks before release.

Three more short reference pages cover methods you meet less often. They are [private prediction](05-private-prediction.md) with an ensemble of teacher models, [correlated noise across training steps](04-matrix-mechanisms.md) for when batches cannot be sampled at random, and [adaptive data analysis](11-adaptive-data-analysis.md), which keeps a reused validation set honest.

The code runs Opacus for PyTorch and Google's `dp_accounting`, and every number on these pages comes from a script you can run. By the end, you will be able to train a private model, defend its settings and its $\varepsilon$, audit it, and write the statement that goes out with it.
