# Differential privacy in machine learning

This part is for anyone who trains, ships or reviews a model under differential privacy (DP). It needs the Gaussian mechanism and composition from [Part 3](../part03-dp-foundations/00-introduction.md) and an accountant from [Part 4](../part04-privacy-accounting/00-introduction.md). Audits also reuse the [membership-inference attack](../part02-attacks/02-membership-inference.md) of Part 2.

Every private training method has two halves. The privacy half is computed. The main algorithm is stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with clipped per-example gradients and Gaussian noise, called DP-SGD. Its accountant derives $\varepsilon$ from the noise multiplier, the sampling rate and the number of steps alone. The utility half, accuracy, is measured, not proved. So each chapter asks two questions: is the $\varepsilon$ correct, and is the accuracy at that $\varepsilon$ good enough?

The chapters follow the decisions of a project, and their order matters. First choose the privacy unit, a record or a person, as [User-level differential privacy](07-user-level.md) explains. Then fix the budget, a privacy level $\varepsilon$ and a small slack $\delta$, with [Choosing ε](09-choosing-epsilon.md). Only then choose the method. That is usually [DP-SGD](02-dp-sgd.md), or for a convex model one of the methods of [Private empirical risk minimization](01-dp-erm.md). Sometimes it is better to answer queries privately, as in [Private prediction](05-private-prediction.md), or to fine-tune a public model, as in [Public data and private fine-tuning](06-public-data.md). When batches cannot be sampled at random, follow the regularized leader (FTRL) with correlated noise takes over, in [DP-FTRL and matrix factorization](04-matrix-mechanisms.md).

Next, pay for the search over settings with [Hyperparameter tuning and private selection](03-hyperparameter-tuning.md). Finally, check which groups lose accuracy with [Disparate impact](08-disparate-impact.md), and test the model with planted records in [Auditing private training](10-auditing.md). The two commonest mistakes are picking the method before the unit and the budget, and treating the accountant's $\varepsilon$ as the end of the work.

[Adaptive data analysis](11-adaptive-data-analysis.md) stands apart. It uses the same noise to keep a reused validation or test set honest, which protects conclusions rather than people.

The tools are Opacus for PyTorch, TensorFlow Privacy, and JAX Privacy, with Google's `dp_accounting` or Microsoft's `prv_accountant` for accounting. The running examples, a logistic regression and a small neural network, come with scripts that reproduce every number. By the end, you will be able to train a private model, defend its settings and its $\varepsilon$, and audit it before you ship it.
