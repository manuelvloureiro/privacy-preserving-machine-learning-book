# Privacy accounting

Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) trains a model through repeated updates. Differential privacy (DP) adds noise to each update; this method is DP-SGD. An **accountant** tracks each step's privacy cost and converts the total to a guarantee $(\varepsilon,\delta)$ for the full run. Part 3's composition theorems can give loose bounds.

In the running example, each of 10,000 DP-SGD steps samples records with probability 0.01 and adds noise with multiplier 1. At $\delta=10^{-5}$, advanced composition gives $\varepsilon=24{,}379.55$. Google's privacy-loss-distribution accountant in `dp_accounting` gives 6.19. Report the accountant, sampler and neighbouring relation with each result; all three affect the bound.

Start with [choosing an accountant](09-choosing-an-accountant.md), which matches accounting assumptions to an implementation, and [calibrating a DP-SGD run](10-calibrate-dp-sgd.md), which selects noise for Part 5's Adult-data training run. The full chapters on [amplification by subsampling](05-subsampling.md) and [Rényi differential privacy](01-renyi-dp.md) explain the two main tools: random sampling can reduce privacy cost, and Rényi differential privacy (RDP) provides a curve that many libraries track.

The reference pages cover [RDP conversion](02-rdp-conversions.md), [numerical accounting](06-numerical-accounting.md) and [Gaussian differential privacy](04-gaussian-dp.md), which interprets guarantees as limits on attacks. They also cover [adaptive composition](08-adaptive-composition.md) for budgets chosen during training, [concentrated differential privacy](03-concentrated-dp.md) for analytics ledgers and [shuffling and iteration](07-shuffling-iteration.md) for other amplification methods. Code using Opacus and `dp_accounting` produces every number. By the end, you can calibrate a DP-SGD run, account for it with two libraries and report its privacy parameters.
