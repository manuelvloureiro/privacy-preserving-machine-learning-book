# Privacy accounting

A model trained with stochastic gradient descent (SGD) under differential privacy (DP), the method called DP-SGD, takes thousands of noisy steps, each on a random batch. The guarantee you report is a privacy level $\varepsilon$ with a small slack $\delta$. The composition theorems of Part 3 are far too loose for thousands of steps. An **accountant** does better, because it tracks a richer summary of each step's privacy cost that composes tightly, and converts the total to $(\varepsilon, \delta)$ once, at the end.

One DP-SGD run is the running example. Each step samples each record with probability 0.01 and adds noise with multiplier 1. After 10,000 steps, advanced composition certifies $\varepsilon = 24{,}379.55$ at $\delta = 10^{-5}$. For the same run, the privacy loss distribution accountant of Google's `dp_accounting` certifies 6.19. So an $\varepsilon$ means nothing until you name the accountant that produced it, and the sampler and the neighbouring relation it assumed.

The part starts with the two jobs. [Report and review an ε claim](09-choosing-an-accountant.md) says what a privacy statement must contain, which accountant to use, how to choose $\delta$, and how to check a number someone else hands you. [Calibrate and account a DP-SGD run](10-calibrate-dp-sgd.md) is a workflow. It turns a target $\varepsilon$ into a noise multiplier for the Adult run that Part 5 trains, cross-checks it with a second accountant, and handles early stopping and restarts.

Two full chapters explain what those jobs rely on. [Amplification by subsampling](05-subsampling.md) shows how much a random batch helps, provided the data loader samples the way the accountant assumes, which is the costliest mistake in practice. [Rényi differential privacy](01-renyi-dp.md) is the curve that most libraries track and report.

The rest are short reference pages, in the order you are likely to meet them. The first three finish the DP-SGD story: the [conversion from Rényi DP](02-rdp-conversions.md), [numerical accounting](06-numerical-accounting.md) with privacy loss distributions, and [Gaussian DP](04-gaussian-dp.md), which reads a guarantee as a limit on attacks. Then come [adaptive composition](08-adaptive-composition.md) for budgets decided while training runs, [concentrated DP](03-concentrated-dp.md) for analytics ledgers, and [shuffling and iteration](07-shuffling-iteration.md).

The code uses the accountants of Opacus and `dp_accounting`, and every number comes from a snippet or a script you can run. By the end, you will be able to calibrate a DP-SGD run, account for it with two libraries, and report $\varepsilon$ with everything someone else needs to reproduce it.
