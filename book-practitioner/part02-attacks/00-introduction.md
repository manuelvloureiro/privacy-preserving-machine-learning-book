# Threat models and attacks

This part describes attacks for assessing models and data releases before release. These tests complement differential privacy: they can reveal implementation errors, but cannot prove a privacy guarantee. Each attack is framed as a game in which an adversary guesses a secret from a model or release. Its score measures improvement over a baseline that does not use the model.

Start with [Threat models and privacy games](01-threat-models.md), then read [Membership inference](02-membership-inference.md). Choose subsequent chapters by the secret at risk. [Memorization and extraction](05-memorization-extraction.md) tests whether a trained model reproduces text; [Gradient inversion](06-gradient-inversion.md) tests whether a federated-learning update reveals a client's input. Two reference pages cover less common attacks: [attribute inference](03-attribute-inference.md) targets a person's sensitive field, and [property inference](04-property-inference.md) targets a fact about the training population.

For released data, read the final three chapters in order. [Linkage](07-linkage-reidentification.md) can identify records without names. [Syntactic anonymity](08-syntactic-anonymity.md) can reveal sensitive values even when records appear in groups. [Reconstruction attacks](09-reconstruction.md) can recover a dataset from accurate aggregates. Removing names or releasing only aggregates does not guarantee anonymization. Part 3 introduces [differential privacy](../part03-dp-foundations/00-introduction.md), which gives a formal guarantee for releases.

For each membership audit, report the true positive rate at a low false positive rate, such as one in a thousand. Accuracy and area under the curve can obscure risk in this region. Risk often concentrates in outliers, duplicates and people with unique attribute combinations, so an average can hide exposed records. A failed attack provides only a lower bound on leakage.

The workflow [Pre-release privacy risk assessment](10-risk-assessment.md) sets blocking thresholds, compares audit results with them and records the release decision. By the end of the part, you can specify a threat model, run a membership audit, test a table for unique records and assess release risk for a model or dataset.
